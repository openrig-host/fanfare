B3 Love

November 14, 2026 · By Dave Waugh · Fanfare 1.7 & Live Organ

I've been attempting to get decent B3 sounds out of my gear since the 80s, when I'd take three simple organ tones on a Kawai K1 and detune them in such a way that the K1 joystick would move from low detune (cheapie slow Leslie) to wider detune (sorta kinda fast Leslie). My Roland Juno-106's Chorus I and II could also do sorta kinda Leslie switching. It wasn't great, but it beat straight LFO pitch warble.

It's 2026, and we've been spoiled by great VST-based B3 emulations for 25 years, ever since the awesome and bizarrely discontinued Native Instruments B4. Onboard hardware B3 emulations are now near-standard: even my modest Yamaha CK88 has 9 drawbars and a serviceable Leslie effect. VST B3s like Cherry Audio's Blue3 sound great in a mix; some can be taxing on the system—like the IK Hammond B-3X—but if your laptop can manage it, they're worth it.

Building a Workhorse Middle Ground

This weekend I wanted to make a middle ground. I have the IK B-3X and Cherry Audio on my gigging laptop, but I wanted a workhorse B3 sim built right into Fanfare that would be lightweight and would sit naturally in a band context, particularly as part of a live stack.

In live performance I'll often run piano or Wurli on one channel, B3 on a second, and ensemble strings on a third, fading them in and out as needed. When the organ is part of a multi-layer rig like that, you don't want it eating half your CPU budget just to sit behind a verse.

What's Inside Fanfare 1.7's B3

So, Fanfare 1.7 features a new, gig-ready B3 sim. The drawbars are CC-assignable, naturally, and Leslie settings, compression, drive, reverb, and delay are all adjustable right on the panel.

Hybrid VST3 Inserts

The Leslie effect I've got going is (in my opinion) decent. However, if you have a favorite rotary speaker or saturation/distortion VST around, I've provided two onboard VST3 insert slots right inside the B3 sim (SAT VST and LESLIE VST) to swap out my internal Leslie and distortion effects. Hitting Byp turns off your VST and brings Fanfare's internal DSP right back on.

The Ping-Pong Delay Feature

A ping-pong delay is also onboard. It's there because:

1. It's cool.
2. I'll be playing a series of gigs this coming week which require me to play an organ solo (Chest Fever), which I can sort of play, but if I crank the delay enough no one can exactly tell what I'm playing, or not.

In essence, the delay knob is electronically jamming my own solo. This is a feature, not a bug.

Also in 1.7: 90s Shimmer for BLEEP-216

Fanfare 1.7 also brings 20-bank, 64-frame wavetables to our native BLEEP-216 analog synth, allowing deliberately 90s bell-and-pad shimmery sounds if you find yourself playing Mariah Carey ballads.
